505 Pre Season at WRSC
by Barney Harris
TEAM SPOT
505 USA 8643

The Chespeake Bay is notorious for its hot, windless summers, jelly
fish, and murky brown phisteria [sp??] tainted water. We in the 505
fleet have found the spring and fall are the best [only] decent sailing
on the bay, and this years WRSC 505 fleet schedule reflects it. The
weather may be a little chilly, raining, and cold - but the sole
meterorolgical indicator of a day's value for sailing in my book is wind
velocity. The spring will typically produce good breeze from a weather
system or locally generated, little on the water or on the road traffic,
clearer water and no jellyfish (sorry - I'm from New York, and we call
'em jellyfish).

The 505 fleet has had three spring practice events thus far on March
27th and April 10th and 25th. On March 27th we fielded 7 boats, and
conducted 8 or so short races in the river, using channel markers for
turning marks. We arranged to finish each race off of the WRSC to allow
us to rotate crews. What a great way to shake the cobwebs out! This
event was very successful from a promotional standpoint as we took
a total of 8 new prospects out sailing.

For April 10th, we were only able to attract 4 boats - of which three
actually made it onto the water for some great practice in a solid 15
kts. We used rabbit starts in the mouth of the river and raced short
triangles. We raced until my crew for the day Allan Freedman smashed his
knee on a tack at the weather mark, forcing us to retire. Fortunately,
my regular crew, Jesse Falsone showed up- and we went back out in a 20
- 24 kts southerly. It was now raining, air temperature of 47 deg f, and
water of 50 deg f… but hey, we had the whole bay to ourselves.

On April 25th, seven 505s sailed short races in the mouth of the river.
Macy Nelson and son Nick were kind enough to run a crash boat and set
a short triangular course. One interesting twist, Macy had the faster
boats start the final few races several minutes behind the others, which
brought most boats near to each other for the finish. Ali Meller sailed
from his home port of SSA in Annapolis to WRSC single handed in the
morning - 1 hour 45 minutes dock to dock - and met up with Finn sailor
Craig Coltharpe, who drove to WRSC in a car. Jesse Falsone noted that
Craig, who has only limited 505 experience, did a "… good job as
witnessed by Ali's noticeable lack of excited comment at mark
roundings." Tom Price brought daughter Sarah (14) out as crew; new WRSC
member Clayton James sailed with long time crew Les Crane; Peter
Mignerey defied insurmountable obstacles to sail with new prospect
Arnaud Lucas, who is from France. Peter commented that "…we had some
funny linguistic break-downs such as when I said 'gybe set' and he
thought I meant 'jib set." Others included new WRSC members and father
and son team Steve and Josh Leiberman, Rene de la Rie with Todd Johnson,
and myself with self proclaimed supercrew Jesse Falsone.

Despite the solid turn out and great time, we did miss several regulars.
Lauren Abramson and Mike Breton are both knee deep in boat refinishing
projects which threaten to keep them out of action for a good part of
the year - bad timing guys - I say if its not ready when the sailing
season starts entomb it in duct tape and defer till winter. Monty
Shumpert is another - although we have seen his boat out, being sailed
by Macy Nelson, Monty has not made an appearance. Finally, our fleet
captain Allan Freedman's thoughts of sailing were crushed by the iron
fist of his household's management. Hopefully he can recover by the
Spring Regatta.
